Privacy policy

Last updated: October 26, 2026 · Applies to Android v1.15.0 and this website

MigraineGuard helps you track your wellbeing. This policy explains what stays on your phone, what optional features send to other services, and how you control that data.

The important choices

Google sign-in, Health Connect permission and Drive backup are separate decisions. Cloud AI uses your own API keys. Drive backups include those keys in portable form; they are not separately password-encrypted.

Who operates the app

MigraineGuard is an independent Android project maintained by the developer identified as jdp323. Samsung, Google, Fitbit, Groq and OpenRouter do not operate this app. For privacy requests, use the developer support email displayed on MigraineGuard's Google authorization screen. Do not include API keys or health records in an initial support message.

Storage on your phone

Account data is stored in separate local databases and preference files. API key values in local settings are encrypted using Android Keystore. This does not mean that every journal database field is separately encrypted by the app; Android's device protection remains important. OAuth access tokens used for Drive are kept in memory. A device-bound encrypted identity remembers your selected account across app restarts; it contains no Google ID/access/refresh token. Explicit sign-out removes that remembered session and invalidates quick water actions, while retaining your account-local records. Notification actions add the chosen 1/2/3 glasses through local, short-lived duplicate-prevention receipts. The remembered identity and receipts are excluded from portable backups.

Health Connect and connected devices

The app reads Health Connect only after Android grants the relevant permissions. Samsung Health, Garmin Connect, Fitbit / Google Health or another compatible source app must first share its records with Health Connect. New profiles use all Health Connect sources; saved source preferences remain editable. Optional movement alerts can separately request background Steps access on supported devices to skip a prompt after recent shared activity. Without usable data, they are timed break reminders, not sitting detection. The app does not directly stream watch sensors, log in to Samsung Health or read your whole Google Fit account. You can revoke each health permission in Android Settings. Health Connect's underlying readings are not copied into the Drive backup.

Health data is used for your tracking and requested wellness features. MigraineGuard does not sell health data, use it for advertising, or send it to an advertising network. It is not used to make eligibility decisions about employment, credit or insurance.

Optional Google Drive backup

When you authorize backup, MigraineGuard requests drive.appdata and account-email access. It stores one current account backup in Google's hidden application-data folder. This permission is for this application's app-data storage; it does not request general access to your ordinary Drive documents.

The snapshot includes your profile and birthday, measurements, goals, preferences, setup status, all configured AI provider keys/model settings, and the seven journal categories: migraine, nutrition, water, workouts, weights, medications and AI sessions. Body progress photos, notes and measurements are included only when you separately enable Include body photos in my Google Drive backup. Meal/equipment photo files and raw Health Connect records are excluded. A current snapshot has a 20 MB limit and an oversized save fails visibly. This can include sensitive information you entered into journal or conversation text.

Backup uses HTTPS and Google's account/storage protections. To allow restoration on another phone, API keys are stored as portable values inside the backup JSON. The cloud backup is not separately password-encrypted or end-to-end encrypted by MigraineGuard. Anyone able to access the backup through your account or a compromised app authorization could obtain its contents and keys. Restored keys are encrypted again in that phone's local settings.

Authorized backup observes saved changes and attempts to save before sign-out. Network failure, Android process termination, quotas or account restrictions can prevent completion; use the completed Back up now status to check. It is one snapshot, not a guaranteed archival or multi-device synchronization service. Standard and Preview builds using the same Google OAuth project should not be assumed to have independent cloud backups.

Body photos and manual exports

Selected body images are resized and JPEG re-encoded locally; original EXIF/location metadata and source URIs are not retained. The normalized photo is stored in the selected account’s private database. Original gallery files are not changed. Local body progress is the default; cloud inclusion requires the separate photo-backup switch and connected Drive. No developer photo or health-record server receives these images.

Settings → Data can save a readable ZIP with structured JSON, eight CSV tables and body JPEGs, or a password-encrypted .mgbak recovery file. The ZIP is not encrypted. Manual formats exclude configured provider keys, Google tokens, raw Health Connect records and meal/equipment photo files. Journal text and AI conversation contents remain private data. The Android picker chooses the destination, including any cloud document provider you select. Encrypted recovery has a 20 MB limit; retain its password and verify copies before removing originals.

Optional AI providers

See the AI prompts and permissions guide for the exact prompt templates, context fields, provider protocols and consent boundaries.

With a configured key, your submitted chat message or food description is sent directly from the phone to the configured provider. Selected-provider mode tries the chosen service, then the on-device fallback. Legacy Auto uses Google Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter and then the on-device assistant, using the separate key configured for each. Supported choices include Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, Groq, OpenRouter, DeepSeek, xAI, Mistral, Perplexity, Together and Fireworks. OpenRouter may route a request to a model provider. Each external provider handles requests under its own terms and retention rules.

The chat's health-summary sharing checkbox is off by default. Turning it on includes the app's health context for that request. Text you type can itself disclose health information, even with that checkbox off. An explicit food/equipment photo request sends the prepared image to the selected image-capable provider (Gemini, OpenAI or Claude). Preparation removes original metadata. Other selections do not silently send the image to another service. A machine-name suggestion does not determine safe technique or load. Merely attaching a photo does not authorize its upload. Automatically generated Insights use the on-device assistant.

MigraineGuard does not run a developer-hosted AI relay or use your records to train its own model. External providers may retain or use requests according to their current policies and your account/product settings; the app cannot promise deletion or no training by every provider. Review Gemini's terms, Groq's privacy policy, and OpenRouter's privacy policy before sharing sensitive content.

Anatomical display choice

The Male/Female selector changes the illustrated muscle map only. It does not infer or overwrite gender, scan a body image or send a cloud request. The bundled vector paths are rendered on the phone; this feature introduces no additional Android permission or Google scope.

Offline illustrations and exercise media

The anatomical selector and its zoom/pan gestures render locally. Male/Female is a display illustration choice, not an inferred personal attribute. Nine licensed exercise GIFs and written movement guides are packaged in the app and play without a network request. Opening an original-source or license link uses your browser only after you choose it.

Optional training AI arrangement

Fitness → Trainer works locally for muscle selection, focused sessions and weekly templates. A separate sharing checkbox and Arrange week with my AI action can send age in years, height/weight, activity level, preferences, allowed exercise catalog, selected/avoided regions and up to twelve comparable recent workouts (up to eight completed sets and optional reps-in-reserve each) to the provider selected in AI settings. No name, email, exact birthday, photos, source URIs, free-text journal notes, medication or migraine journal is included. The provider receives ordinary connection metadata and applies its own policies. The app validates allowed IDs, coverage, time and rest structure and assigns loads/sets/cues locally.

Trainer preferences, test-load references, glass appearance and up to five recent schedules are saved in the account’s private settings and can enter its existing optional Drive/manual settings backup. Saving a sixth distinct schedule retires the oldest schedule; actual workout history is kept separately. Removing a schedule does not erase workout records or independent export copies. No developer fitness-data server or new Android permission is introduced.

Other network activity

If you allow location for weather, rounded coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo to request a forecast. Network services also receive connection metadata such as your IP address. Notifications and reminders use Android capabilities with account-specific toggles, intervals and active hours. A persisted account marker permits generic check-ins after process restart; sign-out clears it and cancels work. Optional GitHub release checks read public metadata without your health records, Google tokens or AI keys. GitHub receives ordinary connection metadata. This static Firebase-hosted website has no analytics scripts, advertising pixels, account forms or client-side health-data collection; Google may process hosting request metadata under its own service policies.

Your controls, retention and deletion

  • Review or revoke Health Connect permissions in Android Settings. Health records retained by Samsung Health or Health Connect are controlled in those services.
  • Pause Drive backup in the app to stop its current backup session. Pause does not delete the cloud snapshot or permanently revoke Google authorization; signing in or reconnecting may check it again.
  • To remove the cloud snapshot, first pause backup, then use Google Drive's Settings → Manage apps → MigraineGuard → Options → Delete hidden app data, if available. Disconnect/revoke MigraineGuard in your Google account's connected-app settings. Revoking access alone may leave stored app data; Google's available controls govern removal.
  • Clear app storage or uninstall the relevant Standard/Preview package to remove its local account files. Signing out alone retains them. Local and cloud copies must be removed separately.
  • The original journal tables merge entries without deletion tombstones, so those deleted entries can return from an old snapshot. New body-progress check-ins retain a minimal ID/date/pose/timestamp deletion marker after clearing the photo, measurements and notes, preventing old snapshots from resurrecting that check-in. A connected photo-enabled Drive save propagates it. Disabling photo backup excludes body data on the next completed save but does not instantly erase older snapshots or independent exported copies. To remove it across copies, stop backup and remove the cloud snapshot as well; check other devices before reconnecting.
  • Revoke or rotate AI keys with the provider if you no longer want them usable. Removing a local key or cloud snapshot does not revoke the provider's key.
  • Manual exported documents and data already sent to AI providers remain at those destinations until you remove them or follow the provider's deletion process.

Local data and the current Drive snapshot remain until removed using these controls. There is no developer-operated central health-record database. The developer cannot retrieve your local journal or privately access your Drive account to answer a support request.

Google user-data commitments

MigraineGuard's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Google account data is used to provide the requested account and backup features. Authorizing backup does not make the backup public or grant other MigraineGuard users access to it.

Children and changes

The current test app is intended for adults, not directed to children under 18. Do not create an account or submit data if you are under 18. We may update this policy as features change and will update the date on this page; review it before enabling new cloud features.
